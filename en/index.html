Online Co-op

From one dispatcher’s desk
to a shared control room.

Run the same line and the same timetable with up to four friends on Steam. Split the stations between you, send pings to say “Look here”, and keep the whole line moving as a team. A morning rush too big for one dispatcher becomes manageable when you call it out together.

01

Meet up with a room code

Create a room and you get a six-digit room code. Share it and others can join — no need to be Steam friends. Steam invites work too, and players can join a room that is already in progress.

02

Split the work

The host assigns stations to players, and each station name takes that player’s color on the map. Everyone’s mouse cursor is shown with their name, so you can tell at a glance who is watching where.

03

Call it out with pings

Hold Q and click the map to drop a ping with a quick message — “Look here”, “I’ll hold”, “I’ll set the route” and more — on everyone’s screen.

  • Co-op is played in Sandbox (free play), on the built-in lines or on Workshop lines (players who don’t have the line can subscribe when they join).
  • Every player needs their own copy of the game. Windows and Mac players can play together.
  • Anyone can give orders — levers, routes, holds, timetable changes. The host controls game speed, incidents, and saving.
  • There is no in-game voice chat, so use Discord or similar alongside it. See the manual (Co-op, English) for how to play.

Features

Everything is on the dispatch desk.

FEATURE 01

The whole line at a glance.

A CTC board shows every train, signal, and cleared route on the line in real time. Where trains are bunching up, where delays are about to spread — the board tells you everything. Click a train to highlight the route it is about to take in orange.

FEATURE 02

Read the diagram.

The dispatcher’s weapon is the train diagram. Planned train paths, delay-adjusted forecasts, and actual performance are drawn on top of each other, so you can see where trouble is heading before it arrives. Working between the board and the diagram to build your next move — that is the heart of dispatching.

FEATURE 03

Take back the flow.

A train failure on the main line, in the middle of the morning rush. That worst possible moment is where the dispatcher’s work begins. Holds, delayed departures, extra stops, skipped stops, turnbacks, terminating short — play your rescheduling cards and rebuild a timetable that is collapsing before your eyes.

FEATURE 04

Your results, in numbers.

Every scenario has objectives and a rating, showing in numbers how far you held down the delays and how many trains you kept running. Same disruption, different dispatching, different results. Try again as often as you like and find the best way to reschedule.

FEATURE 05

Write your own timetable.

With the Timetable Builder you design every train path yourself: dwell times, meets at passing loops, turnback services — all up to you. Watching your own timetable come to life as hundreds of trains is a joy like no other.

FEATURE 06

Start at any time of day.

In Sandbox, pick a line and a timetable, then start from the first train or from any hour of the day. Practice just the evening rush over and over, or watch only the late-night return to the depot — no waiting through long stretches at high speed. You can also trigger incidents wherever you like.

How to Play

The dispatcher’s job, in three steps.

No complicated controls. Watch, decide, give orders. That loop is the fun of running a railway.

1

Watch

Read the situation on the CTC board and the diagram. Where is a delay starting, and where is it about to spread? Select a train to see the route ahead of it at a glance.

2

Decide

Which train goes first? Where do you turn back? There is no single right answer. Your decisions become the timetable.

3

Give orders

Holds, delayed departures, extra stops, turnbacks — carry them out instantly from the orders menu, and the trains respond.

Watch → Decide → Give orders. And the timetable recovers.

Incidents & Orders

What goes wrong, and what you can do.

Dispatching is a battle of wits between the disruption and the cards in your hand. The game features four types of incident and a full set of orders modeled on real-world rescheduling.

Four types of incident

Train failure

A train breaks down on the main line and cannot move an inch until it is fixed. On a dense timetable, a stop of a few minutes turns into disruption across the whole line.

Track or signal failure (track circuit impassable)

A switch that won’t move or an obstruction on the track closes a section, and routes through it can no longer be set. How well can you juggle the tracks you have left?

Section speed restriction

Heavy rain or strong wind forces slow running between stations. It doesn’t stop at delaying a few trains — it upsets intervals and connections and slowly spreads across the whole line.

Section service suspended

Strong wind or a power failure closes a section to all trains. The line is cut in two, and large-scale rescheduling begins as you set up turnback services on each side.

Incidents don’t only strike in scenarios. Use “Trigger Incident...” in the menu to cause them yourself — anywhere, any time. Choose which train fails, which section gets a speed restriction and how strict it is. Design your own “worst day ever” and put your dispatching to the test. Once a scenario’s set time has passed, you can clear incidents manually and keep dispatching until the timetable is back to normal.

The cards in your hand

Stop and delay

  • Hold (keep a train at a chosen station)
  • Hold all trains in one direction
  • Hold all trains
  • Delay departure (add time in seconds, then confirm)
  • Line-clear departure (leaves after its scheduled time, once the next station is clear)

Change calling points

  • Extra stop
  • Skip stop
  • Restore to timetable

For a moving train, whether it can still stop in time depends on its current speed and the distance left. Decide early.

Rework services

  • Terminate short here (make this the final stop)
  • Create a new turnback service
  • Create a new continue (same-direction) service
  • Link to another service
  • Unlink (cancel the next service)
  • Take over a service that hasn’t departed
  • Edit switches and dwell times for the rest of the run

A train you terminate short stays on that station’s track. Getting it out of the way is part of the job, too.

Map Editor

Build your own line.

Choose “Map Editor” on the title screen to build a line from scratch. Place stations, connect them, and the signals, levers, and routes are laid out for you. Your line appears in Sandbox and the Timetable Builder, so you can go straight from building it to writing its timetable and dispatching it. Publish it to the Steam Workshop and other players can play it too.

EASY MODE

Four tools are all it takes to build a line.

Pick a track layout to place a station; a few clicks between stations lay the track, blocks, and signals in one go. Long lines can be drawn in several rows that fold back on themselves by connecting separate tracks.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I play without any railway knowledge?

Yes. The tutorials guide you step by step from the basics, so it’s fine if signals and train diagrams are new to you. Once you’re comfortable, take on lines and scenarios with more difficulty stars. Dispatching together with an experienced player in co-op is a great way to learn, too.

Do I need to have played the earlier game, Railway Dispatcher?

No. Pro is a standalone game, and no story or save data carries over.

Can I build my own lines?

Yes. Choose “Map Editor” on the title screen, place stations and track to build a line, then write its timetable and dispatch it. You can build lines like those in the official maps, though some track layouts cannot be made (for example, loop lines whose start and end are the same station). Please send bugs and requests via Contact (English) in the manual.

Can I share my lines with other players?

Yes. Publish a line to the Steam Workshop from the Map Editor and other players can subscribe to it and play. Lines published to the Workshop can be used for co-op rooms too.

Can I play with friends?

Yes. In online co-op, up to four players on Steam can dispatch the same line and the same timetable together. Create a room from “Co-op” on the title screen and invite others with the six-digit room code or a Steam invite (with a room code, they don’t need to be your Steam friends). Every player needs their own copy of the game, and Windows and Mac players can play together. There is no in-game voice chat, so use Discord or similar alongside it.

Which lines can I play in co-op?

The five built-in lines and lines from the Steam Workshop. Co-op uses Sandbox (free play); scenarios and tutorials are single-player only. If the host picks a Workshop line you don’t have, you can subscribe to it on the spot when you join.

Can I stream or make videos of the game?

Streaming, videos, let’s plays, clips, and monetization are all welcome — no prior permission needed. All revenue is yours. Steam-membership-only streams are fine too.

Can I play on Mac?

Yes. On Steam, the game supports Windows and macOS (both Intel and Apple silicon). Windows and Mac players can also play co-op together.

Which languages are supported?

Seven languages: Japanese, English, Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese, Korean, German, and Indonesian. The manual is available in Japanese and English.
